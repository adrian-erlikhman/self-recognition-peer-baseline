# Generative match: do judges name themselves on text like their own?

Generated by `src/generative_match.py`; numbers come from `results/generative_match.json`. Do not edit by hand.

Proxy for *what judge J would write for prompt p*: J's own corpus response to p. Four similarity measures between responses: **stylo** (negative Euclidean distance over the 18 z-scored features), **word** (word 1-2-gram TF-IDF cosine), **char** (char 3-5-gram TF-IDF cosine), **funcword** (cosine of z-scored frequencies of 164 function words). Permutation nulls use 2000 draws.

How identifying is each measure by itself? Nearest leave-prompt-out centroid accuracy over the 190 responses (chance 20%): stylo 74.2%, word 78.9%, char 76.8%, funcword 77.9%, rf 86.3% (rf = Engine A random forest, out-of-fold).

**Caveat.** In the lineup, J's own response to p is in the same session as r, so similarity to it can act through side-by-side matching rather than familiarity. Only the single-text condition, where J never sees its own answer, isolates generative match, and there only Claude and GPT ever name themselves.

## Reading

**Test 1: no evidence that resemblance to the judge's own answer drives self-naming.** Across the 36 fitted models (per judge and pooled, four measures, two conditions), 1 has p < 0.05, about what chance gives. In the clean condition, single-text, GPT names itself on 79 of the 152 responses it did not write, and the similarity of those responses to GPT's own answer does not predict which: stylo OR 1.52 [0.85, 2.71], p = 0.155, 79/152; word OR 0.75 [0.48, 1.17], p = 0.206, 79/152; function words OR 0.84 [0.53, 1.31], p = 0.439, 79/152. Claude's 13 single-text self false alarms: word OR 1.86 [0.90, 3.84], p = 0.094, 13/114. Pooled lineup, all five judges: stylo OR 0.92 [0.76, 1.11], p = 0.399, 119/722; char OR 0.89 [0.77, 1.03], p = 0.115, 119/722. The permutation test for the prompt-specific component gives 1 of 36 one-sided p < 0.05. The confidence intervals are wide: an odds ratio near 2 per SD in the single-text Claude cell cannot be excluded.

**Test 1b: typical own text is not preferentially self-recognised.** Claude's AUCs for typicality predicting its own hits range from 0.64, 0.41, 0.44, 0.47, 0.30, 0.42, 0.73, 0.61 (all p > 0.1). GPT in single-text is the one case with a signal (word AUC 0.97, p = 0.011; function words 1.00, p = 0.003), but it rests on 2 misses out of 38, and peers' recognition of GPT's text tracks the same typicality (word rho = +0.51, p = 0.001; function words +0.36, p = 0.024): the typical GPT text is easy for everyone, which is identifiability, not self-familiarity.

**Test 2: judges' choices barely follow similarity.** The measures themselves identify authors well (nearest-centroid accuracy stylo 74.2%, word 78.9%, char 76.8%, funcword 77.9%, rf 86.3%). Yet when a judge is wrong, the model it names is the one whose same-prompt response most resembles the text in lineup stylo 31.9% vs guess-null 30.1% (p = 0.115; ref-null 28.3%, p = 0.024) of 630 wrong attributions, and in single-text stylo 24.1% vs guess-null 22.5% (p = 0.020; ref-null 23.6%, p = 0.415) of 659. Uniform chance is 25%; most of the gap above it is already in the guess-null, i.e. in which authors each judge confuses, not in which response. Over all attributions, the named model is the nearest centroid in single-text function words 33.9% vs guess-null 31.5% (p = 0.0005) and word n-grams 30.3% vs guess-null 29.1% (p = 0.001): detectable with 950 judgments, but one to three points. The stylometric classifier does no better as a model of the judges: lineup 32.4% vs guess-null 30.7% (p = 0.012), single-text 26.6% vs guess-null 26.7% (p = 0.653).

**Taken together.** On the existing data the familiarity account gets no support at the level of individual texts: a response that is closer to what J wrote for the same prompt is not more likely to be called J, J's most typical texts are not the ones J recognises, and judges' choices do not follow any of four similarity measures that themselves recover the author 74 to 79% of the time. This fits the rationale analysis: judges apply a stable stereotype of each model, and Claude's and GPT's high self rates come from their stereotype of themselves happening to fit their own text, not from a closer match between the text and their own generations.

**Caveats a reviewer should weigh.** (1) One corpus response per (model, prompt) is a noisy proxy for what J would write; a real generative-match test scores r under J's own likelihood, or samples J several times per prompt. A null here is a null for this proxy. (2) Surface similarity is not the judge's internal representation; the four measures span features, content n-grams, character n-grams and function words, but others are possible. (3) In the lineup, J's own response is in the session, so any similarity effect there could be side-by-side matching; single-text is the clean test but has self false alarms only for GPT (79) and Claude (13). (4) With 13 events the Claude single-text cell has little power. (5) The guess-shuffle null treats judgments within judge x author as exchangeable; responses recur across judges, so its p-values are somewhat optimistic.
