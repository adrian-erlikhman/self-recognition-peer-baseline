\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-preference in LLM judges}
LLM judges rate their own outputs above those of other models
\citep{panickssery2024selfpreference, wataoka2024perplexity, ye2025justice},
and their biases change with question format and answer position
\citep{shi2025positionbias, roytburg2026narcissists}.
\citet{panickssery2024selfpreference} relate this preference to
self-recognition through fine-tuning experiments.
\citet{wataoka2024perplexity} relate it to familiarity: judges prefer text with
low perplexity under their own model, which their own text usually has. CALM
\citep{ye2025justice} compares a judge's score for its own answer with the
scores other judges give the same answer. We use the same kind of comparison
for authorship rather than quality.

\paragraph{Measurements of self-recognition}
\citet{ackerman2025inspection} identify a direction in the activations of
Llama-3-8B-Instruct associated with self-recognition, and
\citet{zhou2025implicit} find that models recognise their own text when it is
paired with another text but not when it is shown alone.
\citet{davidson2024anonymized} find no consistent self-recognition in
anonymised panels. \citet{bai2025selfrecognition} report accuracy near chance
for ten models, with most predictions naming GPT or Claude, and
\citet{laine2024sad} include self-recognition in a benchmark of situational
awareness. \citet{stamand2026sgtr} show that choosing the better of two texts
can produce apparent self-recognition, and \citet{barkhordar2026stylenotself}
show that attribution accuracy for code mainly reflects how often a model
claims authorship. Unlike these studies, we compare each judge's
self-attribution with peer attribution on the same texts, and examine how that
comparison changes across question formats and genres; for open-weight judges
we also compare their answers with their likelihoods.
\citet{khullar2026selfattribution} find that monitors rate their own
earlier actions in a conversation more favourably; our answer-first condition
tests whether this position also helps a judge recognise its writing.

\paragraph{Authorship attribution for LLM text}
Supervised classifiers distinguish model families with high accuracy
\citep{sun2025stylometric, bitton2025fingerprints, rosenfeld2026whose,
zaitsu2025stylometry, russell2026storyscope}, including after the words of each
text are shuffled \citep{sun2025stylometric}. Few-shot LLM judges can approach
the accuracy of such classifiers \citep{dietrich2026splits}. We study zero-shot
judges, as used in anonymised evaluation pipelines.

\paragraph{Text watermarking}
Watermarking methods bias token selection using a secret key
\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark, dathathri2024synthid}. Google applies
SynthID-Text to text from the Gemini app, and Anthropic announced watermarking
of Claude text in August 2026 \citep{anthropic2026watermark}. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}
discusses whether judges could be detecting watermarks.
