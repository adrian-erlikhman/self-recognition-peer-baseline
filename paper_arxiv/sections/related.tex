\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-preference in LLM judges}
LLM judges rate their own outputs above those of other models
\citep{panickssery2024selfpreference, wataoka2024perplexity, ye2025justice},
and their verdicts also depend on the position of each answer
\citep{shi2025positionbias}. \citet{roytburg2026narcissists} find that only
51\% of previously reported self-preference cases remain significant against a
baseline in which the judge compares another model's answer of matched
correctness; our peer baseline applies similar reasoning to self-recognition.
\citet{panickssery2024selfpreference} relate this preference to
self-recognition through fine-tuning experiments.
\citet{wataoka2024perplexity} relate it to familiarity: judges prefer text with
low perplexity under their own model, which their own text usually has. CALM
\citep{ye2025justice} compares a judge's score for its own answer with the
scores other judges give the same answer. We use the same kind of comparison
for authorship rather than quality.

\paragraph{Measurements of self-recognition}
\citet{ackerman2025inspection} identify a direction in the activations of
Llama-3-8B-Instruct associated with self-recognition, and
\citet{zhou2025implicit} report that three models score below chance when a
text is shown alone, in contrast to the paired format of earlier work, and
propose a method that raises this accuracy.
\citet{davidson2024anonymized} find no consistent self-recognition in
anonymised panels and observe that models tend to select the answer they
consider best, whatever its source. \citet{bai2025selfrecognition} report
accuracy near chance for ten models, with most predictions naming GPT or
Claude; several of their models almost never claim a text, and they describe
the opposite behaviour in one model as over-attribution.
\citet{laine2024sad} include self-recognition in a benchmark of situational
awareness. \citet{stamand2026sgtr} show that recognition accuracy depends on
whether texts are presented in pairs or singly, on the conversational role in
which they appear and on the domain of the text, and that choosing the better
of two texts can produce apparent self-recognition.
\citet{barkhordar2026stylenotself} show that attribution accuracy for code
mainly reflects how often a model claims authorship. Both correct for this
tendency in the one-text format, the former by weighting own and other texts
equally and the latter by reporting balanced accuracy with both yes-rates, and
\citet{ackerman2025inspection} compare the probability of ``yes'' on own and
other texts. Our discrimination measure is the same correction, extended to
formats in which the judge names an author. In the related task of separating
human from machine text, \citet{yuan2026crossgeneration} test 15 models as both
generators and detectors and find no systematic advantage for a detector on its
own text. These studies score a judge against the true author. We add a
comparison of each judge's self-attribution with peer attribution on the same
texts, and report it for each question format and genre; for open-weight judges
we also compare their answers with their likelihoods.
\citet{khullar2026selfattribution} find that monitors rate their own
earlier actions in a conversation more favourably; our answer-first condition
tests whether this position also helps a judge recognise its writing.

\paragraph{Authorship attribution for LLM text}
Supervised classifiers distinguish model families with high accuracy
\citep{sun2025stylometric, bitton2025fingerprints, rosenfeld2026whose,
russell2026storyscope}, including after the words of each text are shuffled
\citep{sun2025stylometric}. With 40 labelled examples, one LLM judge (Claude)
matched a fine-tuned classifier on model-family attribution (macro F1 0.996
against 0.991), while a second (Llama) reached 0.387
\citep{dietrich2026splits}. We study zero-shot
judges, as used in anonymised evaluation pipelines.

\paragraph{Text watermarking}
Watermarking methods bias token selection using a secret key
\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark, dathathri2024synthid}. Google applies
SynthID-Text to text from the Gemini app, and Anthropic announced watermarking
of Claude text in August 2026 \citep{anthropic2026watermark}. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}
discusses whether judges could be detecting watermarks.
