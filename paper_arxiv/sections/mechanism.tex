\section{What Judges Use to Attribute Text}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\label{sec:watermark}

Most combinations of judge, text set and format do not pass our test, and the
cases that pass depend on the set of texts. We consider five possible
explanations:
\begin{itemize}
\item the texts contain too little information about their authors;
\item judges claim texts similar to what they would have written
\citep{wataoka2024perplexity};
\item judges claim texts that are likely under their own model;
\item judges claim the texts they consider best \citep{stamand2026sgtr};
\item judges detect a watermark added by their developer.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Information in the texts}
A random forest over 18 style features attributes \EngineAAcc{} of the chat-app
essays (95\% CI \EngineACI{}) and \OWClfPooled{} of the open-weight texts, more
than any judge. To separate word choice from word order, we repeated the lineup
with the words of each essay shuffled. The judges' accuracy falls to chance
(\FSAcc{}; Figure~\ref{fig:shuffle}, Table~\ref{tab:fshuffle}), and no
self-advantage or discrimination is significant. A bag-of-words classifier
attributes \BowShufAcc{} of the shuffled essays, consistent with
\citet{sun2025stylometric}. Claude's provider refused all 38 of Claude's
shuffled lineups with a content filter, and Gemini returned no answer for
\FSGeminiMissing{} of its 38; we treat both as missing data. This suggests
that the judges rely on word order or sentence structure, whereas word
frequencies alone are sufficient for a classifier.

\paragraph{Similarity to the judge's own answer}
For each frontier judge, we use its own answer to the same prompt as an
estimate of what it would have written, and test whether the judge names itself
more often on other models' texts that are similar to that answer. Across four
similarity measures (style features, word and character $n$-grams, and
function words), 1 of 36 tests reaches $p<0.05$, close to the number expected by
chance. When judges are wrong, the model they name is the one whose answer is
most similar to the text in 31.9\% of 630 lineup errors, compared with 30.1\%
under random reassignment. The same similarity measures identify the true author
in 74--79\% of cases.

\paragraph{Judge likelihoods}
For each open-weight judge and text, we compute the mean token log-probability
of the text under the judge, minus the mean under the other three judges. On
every prompt, the text with the highest relative likelihood under a judge is the
judge's own (\OWLikCondAccMin{}; chance 25\%). This is expected, since each text
was sampled from its author, and it shows that relative likelihood is highly
informative about authorship in this panel. Among texts a judge did not
write, the texts it claims are not more likely under its model than the texts it
does not claim: none of the \OWFamTests{} tests is significant after correction,
and the largest effect is in the opposite direction (Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\paragraph{Text quality}
The four open-weight models rated every chat-app essay and every open-weight
text on a 1--9 scale. Frontier judges do not preferentially claim higher-rated
essays, and the lineup self-advantages of Claude, GPT and Grok remain
significant when quality is included as a covariate. Almost all frontier essays
receive ratings near the top of the scale, so this control is coarse. The
open-weight judges show no self-preference: relative to their peers, no judge
rates its own texts higher than other texts (largest difference
\OWQualSelfPrefMax{} points, all intervals including zero).

\paragraph{Watermarks}
The deployed watermarking schemes we consider are designed around secret keys
\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark, dathathri2024synthid}, which the judges were
not given. The chat-app essays were written between 15 June and 31 July
2026, before Anthropic announced watermarking of Claude text in August
\citep{anthropic2026watermark, anthropic2026marking}, so Claude's self-advantage
on these essays predates any announced Claude watermark. Text from the Gemini app
is watermarked \citep{deepmind2026synthid}, and Gemini does not recognise its own
essays. The open-weight texts have no watermark, and the classifier attributes
\OWClfPooled{} of them. The shuffled lineup would also remove a watermark, but
because the judges lose all accuracy in that condition, it does not distinguish
the two explanations.

\paragraph{Stated reasons}
Each frontier judgment includes a one-sentence reason, and we coded all 1,900
reasons (Appendix~\ref{app:rationales}). No reason refers to the judge itself
(for example, ``this is how I write''). Judges refer to every model, including
themselves, in the third person, and 95\% of one-text reasons cite a model's
typical style. The cited features are the same for correct and incorrect
self-attributions (Table~\ref{tab:reasons}): when Claude names itself one text at
a time, it cites hedging in 31 of 33 correct cases and in 12 of 13 incorrect
ones. Grok and Gemini attribute their own essays to Claude and describe Claude's
style as ``polished'' and ``elegant'', similar to the preference for frontier
model families reported by \citet{bai2025selfrecognition}. The reasons
often plausibly describe surface properties of the texts, but each judge applies the same
description of a model to all texts, so the reasons do not separate its own
texts from others'.
